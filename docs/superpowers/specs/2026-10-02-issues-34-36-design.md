## #34 — an operator's rejection sticks

**Today.** `reject_match` (and `apply_reply`'s verdicts, through `reject_in_tx`) appends
`unpair`, author `operator`, for the pairing's `match_id`. The fold turns that candidate
`rejected`. A later machine write for the same payment and document (one `match_id` per
pair, `_match_id_for`) appends `pair` or `propose` and re-activates it. `_machine`
refuses only when the operator currently holds that document (`operator_current`).

**G1. What a rejection is bound to (D1).** The rejection's own `unpair` entry records,
at the moment the operator rejects:
- in `fp`, the payment's snapshot: canonical JSON of `R.fingerprint(facts, kind)` (the
  live row's facts and the expected kind, exactly as `_machine` compares them) and the
  bank's exchange-rate pair (`fx_rate`, `fx_unit`, #35), because a corrected rate is new
  evidence;
- in `detail`, the document's fingerprint: canonical JSON of the facts a judgment reads
  (`kind`, `amount_minor`, `currency`, `document_date`, `issuer`, `counterparty`,
  `document_number`, `recipient`). Not `date_read_at`, not `irrelevant`.

The fold ignores both fields on an `unpair`.

**G2. The refusal (D1).** `_machine` (both `record_match` auto and `propose_match`) finds
the operator's latest verdict on this document in this lineage: the latest operator-authored
`pair` or `unpair` over every `match_id` the lineage holds for the document (a merge moves
the loser's log and ids to the survivor). Auto entries never count, because they are what
is blocked. The write is refused when that verdict is an `unpair` whose two snapshots
equal the payment's and the document's now: "the operator rejected this pairing (<date>);
it is not proposed again unless the payment or the document changes — leave it". A
change on either side lifts the block, and the next proposal is shown to the operator
again. An operator `pair` after the rejection lifts it too. A rejection from before 0.8.0
recorded neither snapshot and does not block, which is 0.7.0's behaviour: the operator is
asked once more, and that answer sticks. Blocking it instead would keep a later-corrected
invoice MISSING forever.

**G3. Judge-due.** `judge_due_state` does not count a payment as due on a document whose
pairing with it is blocked by G2. Otherwise a blocked document keeps scheduling
judgments and making package rounds "unjudged" for nothing.

**G4. What is untouched.** Operator writes: confirming, pairing by the operator's own
words, and "have another look". A rejected document stays free for every other payment.
Store retirements (`retire`, author `store`) are not operator rejections.

**G5. Skill.** The specialist: a pairing refused as rejected by the operator is left
alone. Ellen: `reject_match`, `confirm_match` and `apply_reply` act only on the
operator's own words in this conversation, never on her own judgment. #34 records her
second rejection "as operator" without asking.

## #36 — a 0.00 row is not a missing document

**Z1.** `expectation.derive` takes `zero` (the row's `amount_minor` is 0). For a
classification-derived expectation (rows 3 and 6–13, the shipped mapping or a chain
override) that names a document kind, `zero` makes the tier `optional`. The kind stays,
so a real 0.00 document (a trial invoice) still pairs, and per the spec a tier change
does nothing to a pairing. Rows 1 (exempt) and 2 (a counterparty override, set by the
operator or the specialist) are explicit and kept as set. Rows 4 and 5 (kind unknown)
stay as they are.

**Z2.** So a 0.00 card authorisation is `OPTIONAL-MISSING`, not `MISSING`: not in "Missing
required documents", not counted as missing in the review, searched after the required
items (A5 order). A paired 0.00 document ships in `documents/`, the optional tier's
folder.

## #35 — a foreign-currency pairing is screened by the bank's rate

**R1. Import.** `ledger.parse` reads `exchange_rate` and `exchange_unit_currency` when the
export has them, and validates them by bank-feed's own rules (rate: unsigned ASCII decimal,
≤15 integer and ≤20 fractional digits, nonzero; unit: `[A-Z]{3}`). An invalid or half
pair is stored as none; nothing refuses the import. Stored in two new `bank_rows` columns
`fx_rate`, `fx_unit` (schema 9). Not part of the facts (`FACT_KEYS`): a fingerprint never
moves because bank-feed starts exposing a rate. An export without the columns (bank-feed
below 0.22.0) stores none, and everything else is as in 0.7.0.

**R2. The expected amount.** For a payment with a rate and a document in another
currency: if the unit is the payment's currency, the document should be
`payment × rate`; if the unit is the document's currency, `payment / rate`. Otherwise
there is no screen. The arithmetic uses `decimal` at a precision far above the inputs'
35 digits and rounds to minor units half-up.

**R3. The screen.** `_machine` refuses a pairing (auto or proposed) of such a document
when its amount differs from the expected amount by more than `max(0.02, 3%)` of the
expected: "this USD 7.18 invoice cannot be the EUR 9.52 charge: at the bank's rate that
charge is USD 11.05". In the live data, 8 of 10 correct proposals were within 0.01 and
2 within about 1%, and the two wrong ones were 35% and 54% off. `judge_due`'s FX predicate
(D5) applies the same screen. Operator pairings are not screened.

**R4. Review.** The FX evidence line adds the bank's conversion when a rate exists: "The
invoice is in USD 11.00; the payment is EUR 9.48 (USD 11.00 at the bank's rate)."

**R5. Not done.** Auto-pairing at the bank's rate. #30's decision stands: a
foreign-currency document is proposed, and the operator confirms. This can be revisited
once the screen has run live.

## Testing (pinning)

- G: a rejected pairing is refused for `record_match` auto and `propose_match` with the
  same facts. It is accepted after the payment's facts change and after the document's
  amount changes, but not after only `date_read_at` changes. A pre-0.8.0 rejection with no
  stored fingerprint blocks. Another payment can take the document. The operator can
  still pair it. A blocked document makes no payment judge-due.
- Z: a 0.00 DBIT software row is invoice/optional and not missing in the review or the
  package. A 0.00 row under a counterparty override keeps it. A 0.00 trial invoice still
  pairs and ships.
- R: parse keeps a valid pair, drops an invalid or half one, and accepts an export without
  the columns. Both rate directions are covered. A document 35% off is refused, 1% off
  accepted. With no rate, there is no screen. The fingerprint is unchanged by the new
  columns. The review line shows the converted amount. Migration from schema 8 as v0.7.0
  shipped it.
